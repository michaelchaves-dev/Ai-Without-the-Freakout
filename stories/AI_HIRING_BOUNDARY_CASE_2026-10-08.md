# AI Without the Freakout — When Does Your Job Interview REALLY Begin?

**Editorial status:** DRAFT / developing story — NOT published. **Started:** 2026-10-08. **Publisher:** Subtract Architect Studios.

## Hook
You think the interview starts when the video call begins. But a hiring journey can start with a job search, a website visit, or a friendly recruiting chatbot. **When does ordinary assistance become candidate assessment — and would you know?**

## What we actually observed
A job seeker visiting Extra Space Storage's careers site for a Pembroke store-manager opening chatted with **Sage**, marked **“Powered by AppVault AI.”** Asked about candidate data and evaluation, Sage said it could not provide specifics on storage, described chats as **generally** informational rather than formal evaluations, said formal assessment **usually** begins after applying, and recommended asking the hiring team. The screenshots do **not** show whether messages are saved, linked to applicant records, or used in hiring.

## The important distinction
**Data collection ≠ identification ≠ screening ≠ evaluation ≠ hiring decision.** AI can help candidates navigate jobs faster and make scheduling easier; transparency is necessary to understand where evaluation actually begins. No allegation of secret scoring or bias is supported by the current evidence.

## Five questions applicants deserve answered
1. When does screening or evaluation officially begin?
2. Are chatbot conversations retained, and for how long?
3. Can pre-application conversations be connected to applications or influence evaluation?
4. Who can review, correct, or delete applicant data?
5. Is human review or an alternative interview format available?

## Planned reporting
Follow the applicant journey from chatbot to application, email chain, interview invitations, interviews, and outcome; record dates, disclosures, permissions, and evidence. Seek employer/vendor comment before drawing conclusions. Preserve correspondence privately and redact personal details.

## Closing thought
**The question isn't whether AI belongs in hiring. It's whether applicants can tell when they're being evaluated.**
